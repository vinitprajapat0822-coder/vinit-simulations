Archimedes’ principle · floating equilibrium
ρᵢ Vᵢ g = ρw Vₛ g
hₛ = H × (ρᵢ / ρw)
H = total ice thickness; hₛ = submerged depth; ρᵢ = ice density; ρw = water density.

What should you observe?

1. Ice density increases: a larger fraction of the ice goes underwater.

2. Water density increases: less of the ice needs to be submerged.

3. Ice thickness increases: both submerged depth and exposed height increase proportionally if densities stay constant.

4. Ice density ≥ water density: a freely floating equilibrium is not possible in this ideal model.

*Rope-length convention: this model defines the measured length as the vertical distance from the water surface to the top of the ice. A separate physical rope could require different geometry and boundary conditions.

Worked Example

Given: H = 15 m, ρᵢ = 800 kg/m³ and ρw = 1000 kg/m³.

hₛ = 15 × (800 / 1000) = 12 m
Exposed height = 15 − 12 = 3 m

Therefore, the ice is 80% submerged and 20% above the waterline. In this model, the surface-to-top measurement is 3 m.
