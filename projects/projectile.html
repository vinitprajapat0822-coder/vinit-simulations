Core equations

uₓ = u cos θ　　uᵧ = u sin θ
Resolve initial velocity into independent horizontal and vertical components.
x(t) = u cos θ · t
Horizontal motion: constant velocity when air resistance is ignored.
y(t) = y₀ + u sin θ · t − ½gt²
Vertical motion: constant downward acceleration.
tapex = u sin θ / g
Time to apex applies when the vertical component is upward (u sin θ ≥ 0).
Hmax = y₀ + (u sin θ)² / (2g)
Maximum height above the reference ground for an upward launch.
T = [u sin θ + √((u sin θ)² + 2gy₀)] / g
Positive landing time at y = 0 for a launch from y₀ ≥ 0 with a non-negative initial vertical component.
R = u cos θ · T
Horizontal displacement at ground impact. For y₀ = 0 and same-height landing, R = u² sin(2θ)/g.
y = y₀ + x tan θ − gx² / (2u² cos² θ)
Trajectory equation when cos θ ≠ 0.
umin = √[g(Δy + √(x² + Δy²))]
Minimum speed to reach target displacement (x, Δy), for x > 0 in ideal uniform gravity. Here Δy = ytarget − y₀.

Understand the common question types

Horizontal projection

Set θ = 0°. Vertical fall determines flight time; horizontal speed stays constant.

Vertical projection

At θ = +90°, horizontal range is zero. The projectile rises and returns to its launch height if u > 0.

Same-height landing

For y₀ = 0 and 0° < θ < 90°, T = 2u sin θ/g and R = u² sin 2θ/g.

Maximum range

On level ground with fixed speed and no drag, range is maximum at 45°. This result changes if launch and landing heights differ.

Target-hit angles

For fixed speed, a target may have two launch angles, one tangent solution, or no real solution.

Minimum speed

For a target to the right (x > 0), the minimum-speed path is tangent to the reachable envelope.

Complementary angles

On level ground with the same launch and landing height, θ and 90° − θ give the same range but different flight times and heights.

When this model fails

Air drag, wind, changing gravity, launch mechanisms and terrain require additional modeling.
