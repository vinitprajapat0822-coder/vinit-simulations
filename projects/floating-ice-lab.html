Physics derivation

1

Condition for floating equilibrium
The ice is at rest, so the upward buoyant force equals its weight.

FB = mg
2

Apply density = mass / volume
Let A be the horizontal area, t the total thickness and x the submerged depth.

ρice · A · t · g = ρwater · A · x · g
3

Calculate submerged depth
Cancel A and g from both sides.

x = (ρice / ρwater) × t
4

Calculate the rope length
The water surface is above the submerged portion and below the top surface of the ice.

Lmin = t − x = t × (1 − ρice / ρwater)

For the given values, the minimum rope length is 3.00 m.

Assumptions: uniform ice, uniform water density, freely floating ice, negligible rope thickness and bucket size, and no waves or acceleration.

Force analysis

The force arrows in the experiment represent these forces:

Weight: W = ρice × A × t × g

Gravity acts vertically downward through the centre of mass.

Buoyancy: FB = ρwater × Vdisplaced × g

Buoyancy acts upward. Its magnitude equals the weight of displaced water.

Net force = FB − W = 0

Why do the forces balance? The ice is floating at rest. If it were accelerating vertically, the net force would not be zero.

Check your understanding
